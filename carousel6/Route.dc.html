PLATE 08 — BUILD ROUTE
Prove it small, then go full size

The riskiest parts of this design are the two seals and the queue temperature — not the pod. So the cheapest first build skips custom pods entirely and runs on stock Storz & Bickel dosing capsules in a reducer sleeve. That gets a working carousel in front of you for the price of a disc and two seals; the full-size pod arrives once the mechanism is known to work.

STAGE 01
One station, no disc
A fixed plate on the bayonet collar carrying a single pocket, the floating nozzle and the clamp. Fit it, run a bag, and find out whether the two seals hold at all.
ANSWERS — DOES IT SEAL
STAGE 02
Four stations, by hand
Add the disc, the ring and the cam. Turn it by hand. Put thermocouples in all four pockets and run three bags back to back.
ANSWERS — QUEUE TEMPERATURE
STAGE 03
Motor and sensors
Crown teeth, gearmotor, Hall sensor, plenum pressure sensor, controller. The mechanism is unchanged — the hand is simply replaced.
ANSWERS — INTERLOCK LOGIC
STAGE 04
Full-size pods
Swap the reducer sleeves for Ø30 pods and the riser set. Nothing else on the machine changes — the pocket, clamp and nozzle were sized for this all along.
ANSWERS — FULL BOWL FILL TIME
The two consumables, to scale
The Volcano's chamber is far larger than the capsules built for the Mighty, Crafty and Venty — which is exactly why the pod is sized to the Volcano's own Ø30 screen rather than to a capsule. Route B borrows the capsule as a stand-in, not as the destination.
