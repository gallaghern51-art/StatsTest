PLATE 07 — RISK REGISTER
What could stop this working
NOTHING HERE HAS BEEN BUILT OR TESTED
FIGURES ARE TARGETS, NOT MEASUREMENTS
Vendor documentation for the Volcano Hybrid and the dosing capsules was unreachable from this machine — the manufacturer's own domains are blocked here — so the host figures below come from retailer listings and support-page summaries. The chamber capacity, screen diameter, temperature range and power draw are consistent across several independent sources. Every interface dimension is unverified and must be measured off a real device.
The hard problems
Heat soak into the queue
HIGHEST
Three loaded pods sit 45 mm from a station running at up to 230 °C. Cook them slowly and the session degrades before you get to them. The design answers with a retracting nozzle, a 3 mm still-air gap, a polished deck face as a radiant shield, and three-point pod support. Target is under 60 °C at the queue after three consecutive bags — and that number is a goal, not a result. It needs a thermocouple mule before anything else gets built.
The hot seal's service life
HIGH
No elastomer survives 230 °C indefinitely, which is why the inlet seal is lapped steel on graphite foil. Graphite is stable at temperature but it is soft: the open question is how many open-close cycles the foil takes before it sheds particles into the air path. Testing is cheap — cycle it a thousand times and look.
Electronics next to a hot deck
HIGH
The motor, Hall sensor and controller live on the upper deck, two decks up from the heat, but a gearmotor rated to 60 °C ambient is a real constraint. Add an NTC on the lower deck and a firmware lockout, and expect to iterate on where the board sits.
Fines escaping the pods
MEDIUM
Captive screens hold the charge, but any mesh coarse enough to pass air will pass dust. Over a few sessions that collects in the disc pockets. Not dangerous, but it wants a lip on the pocket and a disc that lifts out for a rinse — both of which are in the design already.
Top-heaviness
MEDIUM
Roughly 380 g added 78 mm above a deck that is already 200 mm up, on a 1.8 kg base. Stable on a bench, less forgiving of a knock — and a full balloon pulls sideways. Ballasting the lower deck costs nothing and helps.
Measure before cutting metal
01
Chamber housing geometry. Bore, shelf height, lug profile, retention force. The single interface that cannot be derived — only measured.
02
Outlet ring face. Diameter, flatness, and how much gasket preload it will take without distorting.
03
Pump curve. Pressure against flow, so bag-fill time at the full setting can be predicted rather than hoped for.
04
Dosing capsule outside diameter, if the reducer-sleeve route on plate 08 gets built. Published figures disagree with each other.
Still open
Does the Volcano's own temperature control behave with a different thermal load on its outlet? Its sensor sits in the heater block, so probably — but that is an assumption, not a finding.
Is the queue target reachable without active cooling? If not, the honest fallback is to load fewer pods, or to accept that the last pod in a long session is not the same as the first.
Can the pressure sensor reliably distinguish the pump running from a draw on the whip? If not, the interlock falls back to a dwell timer and the motor simply refuses to move for a fixed period.
If the power fails
The ring keeps a knurled band and the reduction stays back-drivable, so an unpowered unit is still a hand-indexed carousel. Soft detents hold each station. This is the fallback, not a second product — but it means a dead USB cable does not end the session.
WARRANTY & APPROVALS
The Volcano Hybrid is sold as a certified device in several markets. A third-party part sitting in its air path would need its own safety assessment before it could be sold, and fitting one will void the manufacturer's warranty. That is not a reason to stop prototyping — it is a reason not to sell one without doing that work first.
