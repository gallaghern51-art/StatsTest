Cam development — one 90° step
The motor turns the ring; the ring drives both seals. The disc is only free while both are clear.
The drive
01
An N20-class gearmotor at 30:1 lies flat on the upper deck and drives crown teeth cut into the ring's top face — so the drive costs no height at all.
02
Four magnets and one Hall sensor give absolute station position after a single homing turn. Soft detents hold the station when it is unplugged.
03
A MEMS pressure sensor tees into the transfer plenum — clean air, never vapour — so the controller knows when the Volcano's pump is actually running.
04
Hard interlock: the disc cannot turn while flow is detected. Indexing only ever happens between bags.
—
USB-C 5 V, about 1 A at peak. The ring keeps a knurled band, so it still turns by hand with the power off.
Why four and not six
01
Six full-size pods force the disc out to Ø140. Four keeps it at Ø120, inside the Volcano's own footprint.
02
Hot air travels 32 mm to the station instead of 41 — less inlet-side volume to heat, less to purge.
03
Neighbours sit further apart than they would at six — 45 mm centre to centre against 41 — so the queue runs cooler on a smaller disc.
—
The cost is capacity: 2.6 g a load at full instead of 3.9. Four full bowls is already a long session.
